Desk guide

How to read the desk and turn it into trade decisions

The desk shows you two things: which way the market is trending and how traders are positioned. Neither is a buy or sell signal on its own. The edge comes from spotting when positioning and price disagree, then waiting for price to confirm.

This is a framework for my own analysis, not investment advice. Leveraged crypto trading can lose more than you put in. Nothing here predicts price.

Core principles

  1. Price first, positioning second. The trend indicator and the chart decide direction. Funding, OI and liquidations add context: is the move crowded, fragile, or has it got room to run?
  2. Crowds get squeezed. When one side is heavily leveraged, a small move against it forces liquidations, and those forced orders push price further. Most of the setups below come from this.
  3. Extremes can last. Crowded positioning tells you where the risk is, not when it breaks. Never fade a crowd without a price trigger.
  4. Every trade needs an exit before it has an entry. Decide where you're wrong first, then size the position so being wrong costs a small, fixed amount.
  5. No setup is a valid answer. Most days the desk will say "nothing special". That's when you protect capital.

The 5-minute routine

Read the desk top-down in the same order every time, so you don't anchor on one number.

  1. Trend. Mid-term and long-term scores. Do they agree? This gives you the default direction, or tells you there isn't one.
  2. Crowding. Funding bias and the funding card. Is one side paying up to hold its position?
  3. Leverage. OI pressure and market tension. Is there a lot of fuel for a forced move?
  4. Flow. Position flow over 1D / 3D / 7D. Who has been adding or closing recently?
  5. Liquidity. The liquidation map. Where are the nearest big clusters above and below? Those are likely targets, and places not to put your stop.
  6. Decide. Does this match a setup in the playbook? If yes, mark the trigger and invalidation on the chart. If no, do nothing.

Reading each indicator

Trend indicator Signals · -100 to +100

Measures
Where price sits against its moving averages, how those averages slope, and recent momentum. Mid-term covers weeks (21/55-day averages, 30-day return). Long-term covers months (50/200-day averages, 180-day return).
Bands
≤ -60 strong downtrend · -60 to -20 downtrend · -20 to +20 no clear trend · +20 to +60 uptrend · ≥ +60 strong uptrend.
Use it for
Your default direction. Trade with the mid-term trend when long-term agrees. When they disagree, size down or wait. Within ±20, think range, not trend.
Watch for
Turns. A mid-term score climbing off -100 while price holds a level is often the first sign a downtrend is losing steam. The sparkline shows this best.
Trap
It's slow by design. At ±100 the move is already well along, so don't chase entries just because the score is extreme.

Funding rate Top cards · funding chart

Measures
Payments between longs and shorts that keep the perp near spot. Positive means longs pay shorts, negative means shorts pay longs.
Baseline
Hyperliquid builds in about +11% a year by default, so +11% is neutral, not bullish. The desk accounts for this in its labels.
Bands
Below -5%: shorts paying · -5 to +5%: slight short lean · +5 to +16%: neutral · +16 to +41%: longs leaning in · above +41%: longs crowded.
Use it for
Spotting a crowded side. Use the 24h average in the Read box rather than a single hourly print, which is noisy.
Trap
Hot funding in a strong bull market can stay hot for weeks. It raises the risk of a flush; it doesn't time one.

Funding bias Market gauges · 0 to 100

Measures
Where today's funding sits against the past year and six months. 50 is typical for this coin.
Bands
< 15 strong short bias · 15 to 35 short-leaning · 35 to 65 roughly neutral · 65 to 85 long-leaning · > 85 strong long bias.
Use it for
Judging crowding relative to normal, which matters more than the raw rate. A reading under 15 or over 85 means positioning is unusual for this coin.
Trap
It's a percentile, not the share of traders who are long or short.

Leverage / OI pressure Market gauges · 0 to 100

Measures
How much open interest there is now against the past year (Binance, in coins, so price moves don't distort it).
Bands
< 35 low · 35 to 65 neutral · 65 to 85 elevated · > 85 high, a lot of leverage in the system.
Use it for
Sizing the fuel. High OI plus a crowded side means a squeeze, if it comes, can be large. Low OI means moves are more likely driven by spot buying and selling.
Trap
OI says how much leverage there is, not which side holds it. Pair it with funding and flow.

Market tension Market gauges · 0 to 100

Measures
One number combining leverage (55%), how lopsided funding is (30%) and how big the latest OI change was (15%).
Use it for
Deciding how careful to be. Above about 65, expect bigger, faster moves and wider wicks: use smaller size and wider stops, or wait. Below 35, the market is calm and range trades work better.
Trap
High tension doesn't say which direction. It's a volatility warning.

Squeeze risk Signals · 0 to 100, each side

Measures
Long squeeze: longs crowded + high OI + price already moving against longs. Short squeeze: the mirror image.
Bands
< 20 low · 20 to 40 moderate · 40 to 60 elevated · 60 to 80 high · > 80 very high.
Use it for
A warning against joining the crowded side late, and a shortlist for squeeze setups (see the playbook). Above 50, don't add to the crowded side.
Trap
It's a score, not a probability. Squeezes need a trigger; a high score can sit there for days.

Liquidation map Liquidity · model

Measures
An estimate of where positions opened in the last 30 days would be force-closed. Red bars below price are long liquidations; green bars above are short liquidations. The tables list the biggest zones.
Use it for
Targets: price is often drawn toward large clusters, because hitting them triggers forced orders that extend the move. Stops: don't put your stop inside or just before a big cluster, since price often sweeps those. Put it beyond the cluster, or skip the trade.
Read it as
Rough zones, not exact prices. Compare the sides: much more size above than below means a move up has more forced buying to feed on, and vice versa.
Trap
It's modeled from Binance OI with assumed leverage. It's blind to positions opened more than 30 days ago and to other exchanges, and it lags up to a day.

Position flow Positioning · 1D / 3D / 7D

Measures
Price change and Binance OI change over closed days, classified into the four standard readings.
Readings
Longs adding (price ↑, OI ↑): trend has fuel. Short covering (price ↑, OI ↓): rally driven by shorts exiting, can fade. Shorts adding (price ↓, OI ↑): fuel for a later squeeze. Long liquidation (price ↓, OI ↓): a flush, leverage clearing out.
Use it for
Seeing if a move is healthy. Agreement across 1D, 3D and 7D is more meaningful than one window.
Trap
OI can't actually identify who opened a position. These are the usual interpretations, not facts.

Long / short ratios Positioning · Binance

Measures
Top traders by position size: how Binance's biggest accounts are positioned, weighted by size. All accounts: the share of all accounts that are net long. Taker buy/sell: whether aggressive market orders were mostly buys (> 1) or sells (< 1).
Use it for
Spotting a gap between big and small traders. Retail ("all accounts") heavily long while price falls is a classic sign that longs are trapped.
Trap
Retail accounts on Binance are usually net long, so readings of 2 to 3 are normal. Watch for changes and extremes, not the level.

Premium, volume, OI / volume Top cards

Premium
Perp price vs spot (oracle). A discount means perp traders are more bearish than spot. Price rising while the perp trades at a discount suggests real spot buying, which is a healthier rally.
Volume
Breakouts on rising volume are more trustworthy. A breakout on thin volume often fails.
OI / volume
High means a lot of positioning relative to trading activity. That's stored-up fuel for a forced move.

Putting signals together

Single readings mean little. These combinations are what traders actually look for:

UpFunding neutral, longs addingHealthy trend with room to run. Buy pullbacks.

Setups playbook

Each setup has the conditions to look for on the desk, the trigger to wait for on the price chart, and where the idea is wrong. If you can't define all three, there's no trade.

1. Trend pullback with trend

The bread-and-butter trade. Join a healthy trend after a dip that flushes weak hands.

Desk conditions

  • Mid and long-term trend agree, both beyond ±40
  • Funding bias between 35 and 70 (trend not crowded)
  • Price pulls back into a cluster on the liquidation map against the trend

Trigger on the chart

  • The pullback sweeps the cluster (a wick through it, OI dips)
  • Price then reclaims the level it broke

Invalidation

  • A close back beyond the sweep low (or high)

Targets

  • The prior swing high/low, then the next big cluster in the trend direction

2. Short squeeze counter-trend

Shorts have piled into a downtrend and are paying to stay in. If price stops falling, they're forced to buy back.

Desk conditions

  • Funding bias under 20, 24h funding below baseline
  • Position flow "Shorts adding" over 3D and 7D
  • Short-squeeze score above 45, OI pressure above 40
  • A big short cluster within reach above

Trigger on the chart

  • Price stops making new lows (a higher low)
  • A close back above the prior day's high or a broken support
  • Bonus: mid-term trend score starts rising off its low

Invalidation

  • A new low below the higher low

Targets

  • The nearest large short cluster above. Take most of it off there; squeezes reverse fast.

Counter-trend, so use half your normal size.

3. Long squeeze counter-trend

The mirror image: longs are crowded into an uptrend that's stalling.

Desk conditions

  • Funding bias above 80, funding above +41% a year
  • Position flow "Longs adding" over 3D and 7D, OI pressure above 65
  • Long-squeeze score above 45
  • A big long cluster close below

Trigger on the chart

  • A failed breakout or lower high at resistance
  • A close below the prior day's low

Invalidation

  • A new high above the failed breakout

Targets

  • The nearest large long cluster below

Counter-trend, so use half your normal size. Hot funding alone is not enough; wait for the failure.

4. After the flush reset

A sharp move liquidates one side, OI drops hard and funding resets to neutral. The market is now less fragile.

Desk conditions

  • A large one-day OI drop (flow "Long liquidation" or "Short covering")
  • Funding back near baseline, squeeze scores falling
  • The cluster that was hit has disappeared from the map

How to use it

  • If the flush was against the main trend, look for setup 1 in the trend direction
  • If it was with the trend, expect a bounce or pause; don't enter late

5. Range no trend

Desk conditions

  • Both trend scores within ±20
  • Gauges neutral, tension under 40
  • Clusters on both sides at similar distances

How to use it

  • Fade the edges of the range, with stops outside it
  • Or sit out and wait for a breakout with rising volume and OI

When not to trade

  • Trend and positioning point in opposite directions and there's no price trigger.
  • Tension above 75 and you don't have a clear invalidation level.
  • The desk shows "Updated" in red (stale data), or the Binance date is several days old.
  • Just before major scheduled news (FOMC, CPI, big token unlocks).
  • You're trading to make back a loss.

Risk and position sizing

Desk signals improve the odds slightly. Risk management is what keeps you in the game long enough for that to matter.

  1. Fix the risk per trade. Risk 0.5% to 1% of your account per idea. Counter-trend setups get half that.
  2. Size from the stop, not the other way round. Position size = amount at risk ÷ distance to stop. With a $10,000 account risking 1% ($100) and a stop 2.5% away, the position is $4,000.
  3. Leverage is just a margin setting. Pick it so your exchange liquidation price sits well beyond your stop. If your liquidation price is closer than your stop, the position is too big.
  4. Put stops beyond liquidity, not inside it. If a big cluster sits just past your planned stop, either move the stop beyond it and reduce size, or pass.
  5. Take partial profits at clusters. Big clusters are where forced orders land, and often where moves stall.
  6. Keep a trade log. Note the desk readings at entry. After 20 to 30 trades you'll see which setups actually work for you.

Worked example: BTC, 25 Nov 2026

How the routine plays out on one snapshot. The numbers are from the desk on that day.

CrowdingFunding bias 4, 24h funding about -5% a yearShorts unusually crowded; they're paying to stay short.

Reading: the trend says down, but positioning says shorts are crowded. That's a conflict, so chasing new shorts here is poor risk. Two ways the playbook handles it:

  • With the trend: wait for a bounce toward resistance, ideally one that clears some shorts and resets funding, then look for a short on a failed reclaim (setup 1 in reverse).
  • Short squeeze (setup 2): only if price prints a higher low and closes back above the prior day's high. Half size, stop below the higher low, target toward the $64–65K cluster.

Without either trigger, the desk says wait. Note the nearer long cluster around $55.4K: a sweep of that level is also possible before any squeeze.

Pre-trade checklist

  1. Which setup is this, by name? If none fits, stop here.
  2. Does the mid-term trend support it? If not, is it a deliberate counter-trend setup at half size?
  3. Is the side I'm joining already crowded (funding bias beyond 15 / 85, squeeze score above 50)?
  4. Has the price trigger actually happened, on a closed candle?
  5. Where exactly am I wrong, and is that level beyond the nearest liquidation cluster?
  6. Position size = risk ÷ stop distance. Is my exchange liquidation price beyond my stop?
  7. Where do I take profits, and is it at least 2× my risk?
  8. Is the desk data fresh, and is there major news in the next few hours?

Limits of this data

  • Lag. Binance-based numbers (OI, gauges, flow, liquidation map) run up to a day behind. Hyperliquid numbers (price, funding, trend) are at most an hour old.
  • Partial view. Funding is Hyperliquid's, OI is Binance's. Neither is the whole market. Aggregated data (Velo, Coinglass) gives a fuller picture.
  • The liquidation map is a model. It assumes leverage levels and only sees positions opened in the last 30 days.
  • Everyone sees similar data. These signals are widely watched, so the edge from them is small. Discipline, patience and sizing matter more than any single reading.
